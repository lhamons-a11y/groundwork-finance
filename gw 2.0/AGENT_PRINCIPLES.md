# Item 65 — What the agent does, what it never does, and what it should do more of

Opened 2026-10-09. Rob: *"what is your feeling? I am new to having software with this much capability, where is it
too much and where we are leaving too much to waste. I'll let you write those rules and then I will approve or
disapprove."*

**RULED (Rob, 2026-10-09: "I accept item 66 as a whole and item 65"): AP1–AP11 and TM1–TM8 accepted as the standard every future item is measured against. W1–W10 is a menu: none is opened until Rob chooses.** No code follows from this item. Approved rules become the standard every future
item is measured against, the way the transparency standard (item 39 U7) and *report statements must be proven* are.

## The short version

**Groundwork learns the money; the person learns the why.**
- The engines learn everything the record shows, anticipate what's coming, and work out the best moves.
- Every learned pattern is shown and confirmed before it shapes a plan.
- The person decides, and moves money themselves.

## Part 1 — The rules

**AP1. Chores automated; decisions taught; money moved only by the person.** Gathering facts (downloads, reading a bill,
matching a charge) can be automated. Deciding, meaning which bill, which debt, how much to save, is shown with its
reasons and left to the person. Groundwork never pays, transfers or moves money.

**AP2. Learn from the record, out loud.** Groundwork may learn anything the user's *financial record* shows: seasons,
yearly bills, price changes, gift times. A learned pattern is **shown and confirmed** before it shapes a budget or a
plan, as in *"Gift spending has been about $600 every November for three years. Set aside $50 a month?"* What's
confirmed becomes the user's statement. What isn't confirmed is never kept (*no silent learning*).

**AP3. Never learn the person from how they use the app.** Clicks, visits, dismissals and time spent never create,
rank or imply anything (Willingness ruling 6, the dashboard rule). The record is about money; usage is about the
person.

**AP4. Facts about other people are asked, never inferred.** Birthdays, a partner's income, a child's school: the
person is asked (*"Is October 14 a birthday you plan for?"*), never told it was worked out from purchases.

**AP5. A pattern must beat chance before it's offered.** Every pattern detector is tested against trend-free data
first (the null-model rule). A detector that fires on noise isn't shipped.

**AP6. The engines compute; the model only phrases.** Every number comes from an engine, with its working. The model
explains, introduces and teaches, and its words pass the voice check. It never invents a figure, a fact or a piece
of research (item 58's rule).

**AP7. The person starts every outside action.** Opening a bank, fetching a statement, reading a price on a site: each
is started by a click (or, later, a spoken request to Assistant). It runs in Groundwork's own window, reads only what
it was told to read, names its source, and is confirmed before use. Nothing runs on a schedule.

**AP8. Nothing about the user leaves the computer.** No third party sees the data. Groundwork downloads only whole
public tables, never with the user's details. It never reads email or texts, and never gets around a bank's check for
automated access.

**AP9. When it can't tell, it says so.** Unknown is shown as unknown (*absence is not zero*). A figure without enough
evidence isn't shown, and its gap is named.

**AP10. Everything it did is visible and undoable.** Every automated act leaves a record the person can read: the AI
log, the fetch history, the review cards, the Historian. Anything it changed can be undone.

**AP11. AI is optional; teaching is the point.** Every surface works fully with AI off (A10). With AI on, the same
figures come with teaching: what a figure means, the question it raises, the mechanism behind it.

## Part 2 — Too much: what not to build

**TM1. Paying bills or moving money**, even "just this once", even with a confirmation step. An error costs real money,
and it removes the decision that teaches (AP1).

**TM2. Scheduled or unattended bank access.** It's blocked by banks' two-step rules anyway, and it hides what came in
(AP7).

**TM3. Deciding allocations silently**, for example quietly shifting a budget because a pattern was found (AP2).

**TM4. Reading the person:** mood, willingness or engagement scores from usage (AP3).

**TM5. Inferring life events from purchases**, such as a pregnancy, an illness, a breakup or a job loss. It's
intrusive even when right, and harmful when wrong. If it matters to the plan, the person says it.

**TM6. Persuasion tactics:** streak pressure, countdowns, celebration effects, attention-pulling motion. The voice rules
forbid cheerleading, and motion is a voice too.

**TM7. Health specifics before item 45's H6 privacy gate is satisfied.**

**TM8. Unsourced "studies show" anything** (AP6, item 58).

## Part 3 — Waste: what Groundwork should do more of

Each would be its own item, opened in this shape. They're listed roughly by value against effort.

**W1. Patterns from the first import.** A large first import (2–3 years) yields seasonal spending, yearly bills,
insurance renewals and gift seasons, each tested against trend-free data (AP5) and offered for confirmation (AP2). It
becomes the backbone of anticipation.

**W2. Price changes on recurring bills.** *"Your streaming bill went from $15.49 to $17.99 in August."* Facts from the
record; often the single most useful line a user never notices.

**W3. An upcoming calendar from the record:** due dates, renewals, annual fees, confirmed birthdays, trip charges. It's
"data before they know they want it" (the dashboard anticipation aim).

**W4. "Why?" on every figure.** The engine's working, explained in plain words, with item 58's evidence where it
applies. This is the teaching goal made concrete.

**W5. Cause and effect you can move.** Sliders where the payoff date, the savings goal or the trip's cost moves as the
person changes one thing. Motion that teaches.

**W6. Reading bills and quotes in the window** (item 44's pattern): a utility total, an insurance renewal premium, a
loan payoff quote. Each confirmed, each sourced.

**W7. Questions in your own words over your own data.** *"How much did we spend eating out since June?"* The model only
turns it into a query; the engine answers; the model phrases it (AP6).

**W8. Refunds and returns followed through.** A return you made, its expected refund, and whether it arrived. The record
already holds both ends.

**W9. A year-end gathering** (not tax advice): the year's categories, totals and documents gathered for whoever does
the user's taxes.

**W10. A monthly reflection that stands on its own** (item 64): what changed, what's coming, and one question worth
thinking about.

to open as items, and in what order.
