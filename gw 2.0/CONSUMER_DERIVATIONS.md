## 1. The discipline — what a consumer is, and twelve things it may never do

**A consumer is a read-only derivation over the record that answers one question, returns its
answer with the claims it rests on, and reports what it could not settle.** It is not a resolver,
not a status, not a learner, not a second ontology.

1. **A consumer never writes.** No derived status, resolved referent, chosen value, effective
   period, ranking or "current" anything is persisted — the no-silent-learning boundary: an
   unsurfaced observation may never persist.
2. **A consumer reports ambiguity; it never resolves it.** Contested stays contested; a dispute
   is *derived and shown*, never settled by recency, position, count or source type (`[-1]` is
   the audit's R4); overlap is detected, never resolved (R7 / F7 / F20); "nothing chosen" is a
   valid and often the correct answer.
3. **A consumer traverses established relations; it never composes across kinds** (the D′.11
   constraint) and never manufactures a relation from convergence through a shared person,
   identity, date or document (§D′.2, E24, F13 / F14).
4. **A consumer reads the endpoint it reaches; it never projects that endpoint's properties onto
   the subject** (C17 / C21): reaching X from O lets it read X's nature claims *as X's*, by whom;
   it does not make O medical.
5. **Absence is not zero.** A consumer tests completeness before judging (the $7,053 shortfall
   that was a surplus): *terms never entered* ≠ `deductible = 0` (APPLICABILITY); an absent
   relation ≠ *unpaid* (D6); no `under_plan` claims ≠ *no members* (F12); `Unknown` is a
   terminal state, not a default.
6. **A consumer's answer carries its provenance** — which claims, by which parties, contested or
   not — and the narrator may voice only what is asserted in the findings record (constraint 3):
   a consumer's *derivation* is never itself an assertion.
7. **A consumer never ranks by type, channel or count.** Independence is between origins, per
   proposition, three-valued (C4); readings of one referent never corroborate (the one-source
   rule, Pc2 / Pc9); a process contributes nothing (O9).
8. **A consumer is one consumer.** It computes one question; there is no master status and no
   master label (§7.7's *three facts consumed by different behaviours — not an ontology*).
   Where two consumers disagree, that disagreement is reportable and is not a third consumer's to
   settle.
9. **A consumer names where its failure can appear and is measured there** — downstream of no
   guard that would absorb the evidence.
10. **A consumer's threshold is null-modelled before it ships** — twice the obvious rule was a
    coin flip.
11. **No consumer may become a resolver that other consumers read as fact** (Q2's resolver,
    fenced since slice 2): a consumer may take another consumer's *output with its provenance* as
    input; it may never take it as a claim.
12. **A wiring hazard is repaired by the consumer that arrives at it**, in the mode the audit
    named, and the repair is a refusal where the audit said refusal (R4: a distinct amount →
    refuse, never `attested[-1]`).

**The stopping rule for this item:** *a derivation that cannot state which claims it read, which it
could not settle, and where its own failure would show, is not a consumer — it is a status with a
function signature.*

### 7A.6 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7A RULED as proposed; the build authorised after one wording guard

*The central ruling is correct: a parse dispute is a read-only consumer derivation over
observations of one artefact / referent; it reports disagreement; it does not resolve
disagreement.* The strongest part: `disputed` is not made into ontology — the dispute is a
question answered from the record, not a property attached to the proposition. Against the
governing question — *what may it say?* the observations disagree, and precisely how; *on whose
authority?* the readings, propositions, correspondence state and their provenance; *what may it
not turn into fact?* a selected value, kind, established correspondence or "current"
interpretation.

**The cases, as Rob read them:** Pd1 vs Pd2 give the two states needed — *a candidate that
these are the same line, and if it holds they disagree* vs *established as the same line, and
they disagree* — without a dispute state in the database, and Pd1 must never upgrade candidate →
established. Pd3 / Pd8 are the anti-majority cases: three observations, two contents, one source
— the two `$44,000`s do not outvote the `$4,400`; **multiplicity of observation must not
masquerade as evidentiary independence.** Pd5's omission is a classification, not a dispute —
absence is not zero. Pd6 / Pd7 keep 7A from becoming a general-purpose conflict engine: one
artefact, multiple readings — not artefact ↔ artefact, not assertion ↔ reading. Pd9: a
human-produced reading remains a reading; the reader's identity does not smuggle semantic weight
back in. Pd10: a retracted correspondence is *contested*, the consumer cannot settle whether these
are one line, and it stops — *unresolved substrate state is an answer boundary, not an invitation
to guess.*

**The wording guard, made explicit before coding.** *"Groups of propositions that are one line"*
risked sounding as if the consumer knew line identity. It does not:

```text
established correspondence  →  established referent / component   (referent())
candidate correspondence    →  candidate grouping only
no correspondence           →  no claim that the propositions are one referent
```

7A reports what follows from each state; it is **never a second correspondence mechanism** —
exactly the wording that later becomes an innocent `[0]` or helper.

**The implementation boundary, protected aggressively:** `parse_disputes` **must not call
anything whose purpose is to establish correspondence.** It consumes established correspondence,
candidate correspondence and `referent()`; it may never do *"these look like the same line" →
create correspondence*. Candidates originate from the already-fenced matcher / reading machinery;
7A merely reports them.

**The `resolution()` hazard is correctly assigned to 7A** — the old failure *read history →
latest value → call it resolved*; 7A gives *readings → correspondence state → disagreement →
provenance → no choice*, and `resolution()` may not read past that layer. A wiring repair, not a
new concept, as the audit classified it — and H4's boundary stands: **no resolver change**; the
reading-level answer lives in the 2.0 substrate where the referent now exists.

**The null model is a correctness oracle, not a statistical measure:** same established referent +
same kind / content = no dispute; same referent + different content / kind = dispute; identical
re-parse → 0, one changed figure → 1; **no `if similarity > threshold` — ever.**

**Disposition — order ACCEPTED; 7A RULED; implementation authorised exactly as narrow as the
record proposes:** one read-only consumer, no schema, no persistence, no threshold, no resolver,
and a fixture / smoke suite whose mutations specifically attempt to make it choose. **7B is not
opened until 7A's build is green.**

## Pass 7B — Independence / the one-source rule

**Rob's question, verbatim:** *when two claims appear to support the same proposition, what
establishes that their origins are independent, and what observations are insufficient to
establish independence?* Opened as a semantic pressure test, **cases first, no `is_independent()`
designed** — 7A has already established *three readings → one referent → one source*, and 7B
attacks the next shortcut:

```text
different reading · different parser · different document · different channel
· different party · different timestamp · different row
        ↓
therefore independent            — none of these follows from difference
```

Two things made explicit before the cases, on Rob's instruction: **independence is not a
strength score** — 7B may say *independent · not independent · undetermined* and may never turn
that into *two independent sources = stronger* (that would smuggle 7E into 7B); and **"independent"
cannot mean "different database rows"** — the whole provenance work exists because two rows can
descend from one origin.

### 7B.1 What is already fixed

| Ruling | Constraint on 7B |

### 7B.2 The shortcut, interrogated — what "different" would smuggle

| "Different …" | Why it does not establish independence | The case |
|---|---|---|
| **reading** | two readings of one artefact are two observations of one thing (7A) | In1 |
| **parser / process** | a process contributes nothing; a re-parse by a newer parser is a reading | In1, In11 |
| **document / artefact** | two artefacts from one origin are one origin (two EOBs from Lakeview Health); distinct artefacts are *necessary for some* independence, never sufficient | In3 |
| **channel** | the channel is how the reading reached Groundwork, never who knows (K5: the user reporting the EOB is the insurer's origin through the user's channel) | In5 |

| **timestamp / row** | recency and row identity are nothing; the same utterance evidenced twice is one origin | In2 |
| **correspondence** | two artefacts or two parties established as corresponding are one; a *candidate* correspondence leaves independence **undetermined**, not false | In7 |

What establishes independence is exactly C4's sentence and nothing weaker: **distinct origins,
each knowing for the proposition, each chain to its origin established** — where *origin* is read
from the assertion's provenance (C1), *knowing* from `derived_strength` (per relationship), and
*chain* from asserted links (C3) and correspondence (one referent = one origin). Everything else
is *not independent* (same origin) or *undetermined* (a chain or an origin not established).

### 7B.6 Ruling candidate — pass 7B, for Rob

> **Independence is a three-valued derivation between origins, for one proposition, and
> nothing weaker or stronger. Two claims are independent exactly when they resolve to two
> distinct established origins, each knowing for the proposition, each with its chain
> established — the origin read from the assertion's provenance, never from who is speaking, the
> chain read from asserted links and established correspondence, the knowing read per
> relationship. They are not independent when they resolve to one origin — through one
> artefact, through two artefacts of one origin, through two channels of one source, through a
> duplicated utterance, through two mediated chains to one entitled party, or through a
> correspondence a party established. And independence is undetermined — never defaulted to
> either answer — when a chain or an origin is a candidate, contested or unasserted. Difference
> establishes nothing: not a different reading, parser, document, channel, party, timestamp or
> row. A process is not an origin and contributes nothing; a candidate is not a claim by anyone.
> The verdict is a set of origins and their pairwise states, never a count, a rank, a score or a
> strength — three independent origins are three independent pairs and no number; what a
> consumer may make of independent origins is 7E's question and is not answered here.
> Independence is never a property of a document, a source or a party: the same two artefacts
> are one origin for one proposition and, for another, one knowing party and one that is not.
> Reader independence for what a document says remains fenced. Nothing is stored; nothing is
> resolved; the 1.x count of distinct source types is named as what it is — a count of channel
> types — and is not repaired by counting differently.**

**If ruled, the build is one read-only function** — `independence(session, proposition)` beside
`parse_disputes`, plain structures; a fixture over In1–In18 with the absences (no `n_sources` /

**What pass 7B leaves exactly where it found it:** about-ness through correspondence (7C) ·
level consistency (7D) · strength and weakest-link (7E) · RA5 reader independence · candidate

*The line: two claims are independent when two different parties each knew, and each said so in

### 7B.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7B ACCEPTED / SEMANTICALLY CLOSED; implementation within §7B.6 only

*7B did not invent an "independence detector". It established what independence means in this
ontology and, as importantly, what observations cannot establish it.*

**Why accepted.** In5 / In5′ expose the trap: *different people ≠ different origins* — provenance
determines origin, speaker identity does not; and the same speaker does not collapse genuinely
distinct knowing into one origin if provenance establishes otherwise. In12 closes the subtler
loophole: independence cannot attach to an artefact, document, party or source as a reusable
property; it is evaluated for the proposition being considered. The semantic unit is

```text
proposition → assertion → provenance → origin → established chain + knowing
```

not *document / source / person → independence status*.

**The three-valued result is essential.** The candidate-correspondence case is the important one:
two origins that *might* be connected, with only a candidate correspondence, have earned neither
conclusion — *not independent* would manufacture a negative fact, *independent* a positive one;
**undetermined is the truthful terminal state**, consistent with the rule that unresolved evidence
stays unresolved rather than being normalised into a usable status.

**7B correctly stops before 7E.** In14 is the fence: three independently established origins
yield *three origins, three independent pairs* and nothing about evidentiary strength — no
*corroborated · stronger · confidence · majority · score · threshold · preferred source*.
Otherwise 7B becomes the first half of a strength engine and 7E a formatter. **7B: are these
origins independent? 7E: what may be said about the evidentiary significance of multiple links?**
Different questions.

**In15 preserved as a regression target.** The old `distinct = {e.source}` is not "a bad
implementation of independence"; it answers a different question — how many source / channel
identities occur in the record. The audit language is preserved rather than "fixed" into a more
sophisticated count, which could still be completely wrong semantically.

**The 7B wiring guard, made explicit before implementation:** *the consumer may inspect the
established provenance / relationship chain to determine the three-valued result, but it may not
establish, upgrade, resolve or mutate any provenance, correspondence, knowing relationship or
independence relationship while doing so.*

```text
established record → 7B reads provenance → follows the established chain
→ evaluates knowing for this proposition → independent / not independent / undetermined

never:   7B sees evidence → creates correspondence → creates provenance → declares independence
```

**Independence remains a consumer answer, not a new ontology relation.**

**Established, as Rob recorded it:** (1) per proposition · (2) origin from assertion provenance,
not speaker identity · (3) distinct origins required · (4) each knowing for that proposition · (5)
the chain established · (6) different rows, readings, parsers, documents, channels, parties or
timestamps do not establish independence · (7) candidate or unresolved chains → undetermined,
never guessed · (8) process observations do not become independent knowing by being separate
records · (9) not strength; no count / rank / score · (10) the consumer writes and creates none
of the relationships it evaluates · (11) failure reported at the claim where evidence cannot
establish the result · (12) the null model is the correctness oracle.

**Disposition — 7B: ACCEPTED / SEMANTICALLY CLOSED.** 7C stays unopened until 7B is implemented
and its semantic tests pass. **The natural next question for 7C is not "how do we calculate
about-ness?" but *what exactly establishes that a proposition is about an artefact when
correspondence is involved, and what tempting paths must explicitly fail?***

### 7C.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7C RULED as written; four semantic edges settled before the build

*The framing needed no reframing. Narrower issues than 7B's, but one important enough not to pass
as wording.* All nine points settled here, in §7C.2 and in the cases Ac21–Ac28.

1. **"Nothing contradicts" is a defined condition, not a hidden resolver.** *Artefact-level
   derivation is available only when the artefact's established concern set for the requested
   identity type contains exactly one identity and no established contrary concern for that same
   type. Candidate, contested, unestablished or otherwise unresolved concerns do not satisfy the
   condition and are not resolved by this consumer.* The one established concern supplies the
   derivation; the absence of a second identity is not evidence that the first is true (Ac22).
2. **"Exactly one" is scoped to the requested identity type.** An artefact concerning O1, E1 and
   P1 is about O1 for the obligation type — E1 and P1 are neither multiplicity nor conflict; an
   artefact concerning O1 and O2 is ambiguous for that type however settled everything else is
   (Ac21).
3. **Correspondence does the work only as established identity of recordings.** The contract is
   mechanical: *P is about X through a chain only if every traversed correspondence link is
   established*; a candidate link stops the chain there — the mixed chain *P ↔ Q established, Q ↔
   R candidate, R → X* yields X as a candidate for P, never derived (Ac23).
4. **Cycles and multiple paths create no multiplicity.** *P ↔ Q, Q ↔ R, R ↔ P, R → X* is one
   component and one derivation (Ac24); *P → artefact A → X* and *P → Q → artefact B → X* is one
   derived entry with two paths listed (Ac25). **Multiple established derivations terminating in
   the same identity do not strengthen, rank or multiply the about-ness result** — 7B's
   multiplicity fence in 7C's clothing; paths are not counted.
5. **Provenance labels are not an ordering of authority.** *Structural / asserted / derived
   describe how an about-ness result is obtained; where independently established paths yield
   incompatible identities of one type, 7C reports the conflict and does not resolve it* — 7D's
   territory, kept out of 7C (Ac14, Ac26).
6. **"Own established participant" is precise.** Structural about-ness is Pa10's relationship —
   an established participant slot of one of the **identity types that about-ness ranges over**
   (obligation · payment · expense · plan, the concerns kinds' object types). A party subject is
   not "about the party" in this sense; `if proposition.subject_party_id: return about(...)` is
   exactly the silent expansion refused (Ac27).
7. **A reading is a carrier of access, not an about-ness edge.** *A reading is a carrier of access
   to its artefact's established semantics; it is not itself an about-ness assertion and cannot
   create or transmit a concerns relation merely because a read proposition says so.* *Reading →
   artefact → artefact concerns X* can work; *reading → proposition that concerns X* cannot, and a
   sibling proposition's asserted concerns transmits nothing to P (Ac28).
8. **Established and candidate paths coexisting — ruled, not chosen by implementation:** the
   established path reaches; the candidate is **reported as a candidate beside it** — faithful to
   the evidence architecture; and a candidate can never turn an otherwise settled result into
   *undetermined*, which would make candidate evidence a resolver (Ac26).
9. The two rejections Rob singled out stand as architectural boundaries: *Harbor is E of O1 ⇒ its
   lines are about O1* — the role → about-ness leak with no valid general rule (Ac13); *P about O,
   O in respect of X ⇒ P about X* — D′.11 stays a separate question, not an accidental composition
   operator in 7C (Ac12).

```text
                    ABOUT IDENTITY
                          │
          ┌───────────────┼────────────────┐
      structural       asserted        derived
                                      /          \
                                  artefact    correspondence

candidate ──→ does not reach      contested ──→ stops       multiple identities ──→ conflict, no choosing
composition ─→ does not follow    convergence ─→ does not infer   reading itself ──→ does not establish
derivation ──→ does not write     derivation ──→ does not know
```

**The question, applied:** the credit-union statement is asserted to concern the mortgage and the
HELOC; line 3 is asserted to concern the car loan. *Do the artefact-level and the proposition-level
about-ness claims agree — and when they do not, what may a consumer say?* Pa7 answered the last

guard's to refuse and never a resolver's to settle.* Ab7's findings left the report itself
unbuilt. 7C now reports the conflict *as found* on one proposition; **7D characterises it** —
from the artefact's side, across its lines, by kind, with every asserter — and stops.

7D is the pass Rob named as the one where 7C must not have quietly acquired conflict-resolution
authority. So the first thing 7D does is say what it is not: **it is not a resolver, and the
report it produces is not a verdict.** A consumer that reports *the artefact-level claim by Rob is
contradicted by three line-level claims by Kim* has stated a fact about the record; a consumer
that concludes *so the artefact-level claim is wrong* has manufactured one.

### 7D.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7D RULED as written; six things the fixture must demonstrate

*Correctly framed; the crucial distinction preserved — 7D is a characterisation / reporting consumer,
not a resolver.* The core definition is right: **an established line-level about-ness identity
outside the artefact's established identity set for the same requested type** — level consistency,
not general proposition disagreement; the negative cases keep 7D from becoming a generic
contradiction detector.

**The edges settled, and the six demonstrations required before acceptance:**

1. **Empty artefact-level evidence is neither consistency nor inconsistency.** The three-state
   distinction is mechanical, never collapsed by `if line in artefact_identities … else
   inconsistent`: *{O1} / O1 → consistent · {O1} / O2 → inconsistent · {} / O1 → no level basis.*
2. **Contested artefact evidence has exact STOP semantics.** *O1 established, O2 contested; P → O1,
   Q → O2*: the report preserves *O1 → established basis exists; O2 → basis contested*, derives no
   level disagreement for Q from the contested state, and does not pretend the artefact's set is
   simply {O1} in order to manufacture one — a line outside the established set while the
   artefact side is contested for that type is **unresolved**, not inconsistent; a line inside it
   is consistent (its basis is established regardless).
3. **Candidate paths never become inconsistencies.** *7C candidate → 7D sees candidate → NOT a
   level inconsistency* — a line whose only about-ness is candidate has **no line basis**; a
   candidate artefact concern is not part of the established set. Otherwise 7D would acquire
   exactly the promotion authority 7C was forbidden.
4. **Type isolation applies at the report level.** The report is partitioned *artefact → identity
   type → {artefact established set · lines by classification}*; an artefact concerning O1, E1 and
   p never makes an obligation line about O1 "in conflict" because of E1 or p (Ac21's rule).
5. **No Boolean.** The report distinguishes **consistent · inconsistent · no line basis · no level
   basis · unresolved** — never `consistent=False` for the last three. And artefact-derived
   about-ness (7C's artefact path) is *the artefact level itself* and is excluded from the
   comparison — a line whose only established about-ness is artefact-derived has no independent
   line basis; correspondence-derived about-ness does count (Lc-g).
6. **Counts are descriptive metadata about the observed report, not an evidentiary primitive.**
   *1 / 5 · 3 / 5 · 5 / 5 inconsistent* all remain reportable without 7D acquiring majority,
   confidence, weight, probability or a recommended retraction.

**The unit and the dependency direction, pinned:** `aboutness()` → established / candidate /
undetermined / conflict → *7D interprets only the established about-ness, with its provenance,
never as a claim* → the report. Never *7D reinterprets participants → creates about-ness*. 7D is
a consumer of 7C's result, not a second implementation of about-ness. The assertion-disagreement
class (Lc-h) stays separate: *proposition ↔ proposition* versus *line ↔ artefact* are different
questions.

**Disposition — 7D: RULED as written; build it.** 7D characterises the disagreement; which

### 7E.2 What a link is — the chain vocabulary, closed

A **chain** is one evidentiary relationship of the value followed by the links of one 7C path.
Every link is **a claim on record with its own evidentiary relationships**, or the value's own
evidence; nothing else is a link. The vocabulary, today:

| Link | What it is | Its strength |
|---|---|---|
| **L0 — the value's evidence** | one evidentiary relationship of P (an utterance, or a reading whose artefact's origin is derived inside slice 3) | `derived_strength(rel, knowing)` — **as given**; the origin link and O5's laundering test live here |
| **`proposition_concerns(P, X)`** | the asserted about-ness (7C *asserted*) | `derived_strength` of its evidentiary relationship(s): the minter-of-X relation — *knowing* for the household member who declared X, *not_knowing* for another asserter |
| **`artefact_concerns(A, X)`** | the artefact-level claim (7C *derived: artefact*, where A's established set for the type is exactly {X}) | the same relation; the reading between P and A is a carrier of access, not a link — it has no strength of its own and adds none |
| **`proposition_corresponds(P, P′)`** | an established correspondence (7C *derived: correspondence*) followed by P′'s structural or asserted about-ness | **undetermined by design** (Kn4) — until correspondence has a ruled epistemology, every chain through it is undetermined |
| **structural** | P's own established participant (7C *structural*) | not a link: the chain is L0 alone |

### 7E.8 The seam Rob named — what a chain *is*, 2026-09-18

Rob's question, before 7F may interpret it for itself: *what prevents 7E from enumerating the same
semantic chain more than once through structurally equivalent traversals — are `A → B → C` and
`A → B → C`, reached by two distinct structural traversals, one reported chain or two?* And: does
"simple" have an exact operational meaning?

**What 7C says.** 7C's unit is the *path*: one per correspondent proposition and about-ness
(`(proposition_corresponds, P, P′) · structural P′` or `· proposition_concerns(claim, asserter)`);
*several established paths to one identity are one derivation with its paths listed; two routes are
two explanations of one derived about-ness, not two pieces of evidence; a cycle is one component
and one derivation.* 7C does not expand routes into hops and does not identify paths by grounds —
it lists one path per `(claim, ground)` of the correspondent's assertion, because `_about_direct`
reports asserted about-ness per evidentiary relationship. **So 7C's path identity does not answer
7E's question**; 7E's unit is finer, and the record must say what it is.

**Ruling applied — a chain is its sequence of grounds.** `(L0, ground₁ … groundₙ)`, each an
evidentiary-relationship id. Two enumerations that reach the same sequence are **one chain, reported
once**, however many structural traversals reach it. Two different claims on record between the same
nodes — a second `proposition_corresponds` between one pair, a second `proposition_concerns` naming
one identity — are two grounds, hence **two chains**, however alike: a link is a claim on record and
two claims are two. Nothing is merged that differs; nothing that is the same is listed twice. This
is not aggregation (no class is combined) and not counting (no chain is weighed); it is identity.
**"Simple"** = no proposition visited twice on a route, so a cycle in a component terminates rather
than multiplying routes, and no claim can repeat on a route.

**The seam was live.** Checked against the build rather than assumed: a correspondence to a
correspondent whose concerns claim has two grounds produced **four chains, two of them the same
ground sequence** — 7C's two paths for that claim (one per ground) each enumerated both grounds.
Exactly Rob's `A → B → C` twice. Fixed in the derivation: `add` keys every chain by its ground
sequence and reports each once; `_correspondence_routes` documents *simple* exactly. Fixture
**Ms27**: the two-ground terminal → exactly two chains (both undetermined, through correspondence;
their terminal links knowing and not_knowing); a second correspondence claim between the same pair
→ four chains, all distinct sequences; a cycle `J ~ Jb ~ Jc ~ J` → three routes to Jb, no
proposition twice, six distinct chains; and a corpus-wide check that no report lists one ground
sequence twice — enforced, not assumed. Smoke s111 gains a thirteenth mutation (*the same chain
enumerated twice through equivalent traversals*), caught. One expectation in the new check was
written wrong on the first run (chains through correspondence are undetermined by design, not
knowing / not_knowing — the terminal link's classes are what differ); the check was corrected, the
derivation was not.

**What 7F inherits, stated:** a chain's identity is its ground sequence; a route is simple; equal
sequences are one chain; distinct claims are distinct chains. 7F traverses ruled relations, not
about-ness chains, and may not reinterpret this seam. Gate 1206 → 1208, 0 failed.

does not become composition because a consumer happens to traverse two relations.* So the pressure
question comes first — **is there a derivation at all, or is a traversal just a query?** There is a
derivation, and it is small: a traversal collapses party referents, classifies each claim's state
(established · candidate · contested), lists every endpoint with its asserter and **selects none**,
answers the reverse direction as a query without minting an inverse, refuses the substrate's own
kinds (which have their own derivations) and unruled kinds, and — for a role — orders dated stances
and says what they say about d, naming the stance it rests on and stopping where they disagree. Its
value is the discipline the next four consumers will lean on: **one hop, every endpoint, nothing
chosen.**

### 7F.2 Two questions, two derivations — and what a traversal is not

The inventory's row names four household questions; they are answered by **two** derivations, one
per question-shape, because they read different things:

#### TS — what a stance asserts, and the one extrapolation, stated exactly

**The stances (RP2, as built).** A stance is a proposition of one of three sorts for one (party,
role kind, identity), with `as_of = t` the date it is *about* (never when it was learned — R3).
*Established* = evidenced by an unretracted utterance; a reading-sourced stance is a *candidate*
(R11), a retracted one *contested* (R6). Only established stances take part in the answer; the
others are listed with their state.

**What each sort asserts — at t, immediately before t, and about other dates.** This table is the
ruling's content; every case below follows from it.

| Sort at `t` | at `t` | immediately before `t` | about any other date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **holds** | in effect | — (nothing: R1, *nothing about since when*) | **nothing** |
| **began** | in effect | **not** in effect (it is a transition) | nothing |
| **ended** | **not** in effect (convention TS-c below) | in effect (a transition) | nothing |

**The extrapolation (the new rule, TR6a made exact).** *A stance stands until the next stance.*
For a date d and the established stances E of one (party, kind, identity):

```text
G(d)  = the stances in E whose as_of is the LATEST date <= d          (the governing set)
N(d)  = the stances in E whose as_of is the EARLIEST date  > d        (the next set)
fwd(s) = in_effect      if s is holds or began                         (what s says about t, carried forward)
         not_in_effect  if s is ended
bwd(s) = in_effect      if s is ended                                  (what s says about "immediately before t",
         not_in_effect  if s is began                                   carried back as far as the previous stance)
         nothing        if s is holds

answer(d):
    G empty                                        -> undetermined      (no stance precedes d: absence is not zero)
    {fwd(s) : s in G} has two values               -> contested         (began and ended on one date)
    A = the one value of fwd over G
    some s' in N with bwd(s') defined and != A     -> contested         (the next stance's transition
                                                                          contradicts what governs d)
    otherwise                                      -> A
```

**"Straddling", exactly:** the answer for d is contested when the governing stance's forward
reading and the next stance's backward reading disagree about d — and only then. A *holds* reads
nothing backward, so it never contests an earlier stance; a *began* or *ended* does, because a
transition says what held immediately before it, and "immediately before" reaches back to the
previous stance under the same rule that carries a stance forward. Only the nearest next stance
is consulted: any farther stance's backward reading is bounded by the stance between.

**Rob's two counter-examples resolve by the table, not by fiat:** `holds @ 1/1 · holds @ 5/1 ·
d = 3/1` → G = {holds 1/1} → in effect; N = {holds 5/1}, bwd = nothing → **in effect, not
contested**. `holds @ 1/1 · ended @ 5/1 · d = 3/1` → in effect; N = {ended 5/1}, bwd = in effect,
agrees → **in effect, not contested**.

**TS-c — one convention to rule, not derivable from the record:** is the role in effect *on* the
date of an ending? The table takes `as_of` for a boundary as *the first date on which the new
state holds* (began @ t → in effect on t; ended @ t → not in effect on t), symmetric between the
two sorts. The alternative — an ending's date is *the last day in effect* — is a household
idiom (*coverage ended June 30*) and is representable either way by the asserter choosing the
date; the derivation must pick one reading of `as_of` and say it. **Recommended: the symmetric
reading**, with the report naming the stance and its date so the idiom's off-by-one is visible.

**What the rule never does:** it does not resolve R5 (two endings disagree → contested on the
dates between); it does not resolve R7 (two identities each in effect → both, the overlap a fact);
it does not read a retraction as an ending (R6 — the retracted stance leaves E); it does not let a
candidate govern or contest (R11); it does not materialise a period; it does not consult
`created_at`; and the governing set and the next set are **both in the report**, so every answer
shows the stance it rests on and the stance that could contest it.

#### (1) The numeric construction — what "remaining" is, exactly

§7G.2 said *the obligation's asserted amount, less directed amounts* and never defined a term.
Every term now:

**The base.** U6 ruled the figures into three kinds, and only one of them is a position on the
obligation by the party that holds it: **P-ack** — *the entitled party's stated position about
the balance, as of a stated date*. The household's *"I owe $4,000"* is F3's testimony of no kind
until asked; the bill at issue is P-ack at d₀. So **the base of every projection is a P-ack
balance claim** — established, dated, by a party whose entitled-party role on O is in effect at
its date (7F). Which one: **the governing stated position S(d)** — the latest such claim with
`as_of ≤ d`, TS's G applied to balance claims (*a stated position stands until the next*), **named
in the report with its date b**. Two established such claims at one `as_of` → **a conflict: no
S(d), listed**. None at or before d → **no S(d)**. Carrying an earlier statement forward to d is
a temporal rule and is stated as one: it is G, the same rule 7F ruled, and the report shows b so
the carry-forward is visible.

*Decision TG-base, put to Rob:* the alternative — a household-asserted `obligation_amount` as the
base of `attached` (the 1.x `gross`) — is rejected here because it is F3 testimony and because
F2's diagnostic is defined only against P-ack. **Where no P-ack exists, both projections are
undetermined: *no stated position by the entitled party*.** The station report still runs.

**The terms.** For a payment p among those 7F's `reach` returns for O:

```text
amt(p)   the ONE established amount claim on p (catalogue payment_amount); several established
         and disagreeing → undetermined; candidate / contested never count
dir(p)   −1 if payer(p) is a household person AND payee(p) is in the referent of a party whose
            entitled-party role on O is in_effect at p's date          (household → E: reduces)
         +1 if payer(p) is such a party AND payee(p) is a household person  (E → household: increases)
         undetermined otherwise — payer or payee not established, or the payee's / payer's role
            not_in_effect / undetermined / contested at p's date
when(p)  the payment's date: for `attached`, p's established occurred_on claim (catalogue);
         for `confirmed`, the qualifying acknowledgment's as_of (E's own date)
net      p is EXCLUDED from every figure when it is the object of an established
         payment_counteracts (counteracted) or its subject (the counteraction); a candidate /
         contested counteracts claim excludes nothing
```

**The figures, as arithmetic:**

```text
attached(d)  = S(d).value − Σ dir(p)·amt(p)   over p: attachment established, not netted,
                                                       b < when_attached(p) ≤ d
confirmed(d) = S(d).value − Σ dir(p)·amt(p)   over p: DISCHARGED (RG), not netted,
                                                       b < when_confirmed(p) ≤ d
remaining(d) = confirmed(d)                    by name — nothing else is called remaining
```

Payments dated at or before b are presumed inside E's stated position and are not subtracted
again — that is what taking the statement as base means, and it is the only way the F2
diagnostic can exist. **A figure is undetermined — with the reason and the payment named —
whenever S(d) is absent or conflicted, or any payment in its range has no established amount,
direction or date.** Undetermined propagates; it is never zero and never an exclusion. **A
negative result is reported as it comes** — E's figures saying E owes the household — with no
floor, no sign interpretation and no *credit* status; flooring would be a resolver.

So `confirmed` measures exactly: **E's last stated position, moved only by E's own dated
acknowledgments since that position, at the amounts and directions the household's claims give
those payments.** It is not a trusted balance; it is that projection, named.

## Pass 7I — Disposition: the reconstruction

**The question, applied:** *Riverbend Hospital closed the $3,000 plan and opened a new one at $250 a month —
where did the $525 I was behind go? NewBank paid Harbor off and I owe NewBank now — is the Harbor
mortgage gone? The collection agency says $3,300 and the hospital said $3,000 — where did $300
come from?* §5 stated the invariant — *whenever economic terms change, every amount whose economic
treatment changes must have an explicit, reconstructable disposition* — and finding 4 showed it
belongs to no anchoring: it is a requirement to be stated and enforced on its own, whichever
representation the household chose (B1: the same obligation with new terms; B2: a new obligation
that replaces the old). The disposition cases attacked it with worked amounts and produced seven
findings and **a blocker**: *do not introduce an enum for disposition until its semantic scope is
resolved* — the outcome of the obligation, the creditor's accounting treatment, or a defined
abstraction over both; the bad-debt write-off is the boundary test. So **no disposition kind is
ruled**, and B7 fenced the invariant's disposition to *the invariant's consumer — a separate claim
of a different shape, not a field on the relation.* 7I is that consumer — and the pressure question
is sharper than in any pass so far: **what can a consumer of dispositions do when no disposition
can yet be recorded?**

The answer the sources give: **the check, not the vocabulary.** Finding 7 — *relocation requires a
destination, and the check requires origination too; without the first, §5 is a declaration rather
than a test; without the second, the test cannot balance* — and case 8's sum (`destination 3,300 =
relocated 3,000 + originated 300`) already say what 7I is: **the reconstruction test**. It takes the
amounts the record already establishes — the governing P-ack positions before and after the
transition, and the discharged payments between them, all of it 7G's — and reports what balances
and **what nothing in the record explains**: the *unaccounted* amount, named, dated, sourced, never
zeroed, never a fifth number. When the vocabulary is ruled, 7I reads those claims and the
unaccounted amount shrinks; **the shape does not change**. Today it is honest about the gap, which
is what 8.1-a asked for — *the user must be able to reconstruct* — and what 8.1-b measures.

### 7I.2 The reconstruction — what it computes, and what it refuses

**The transition.** The household names an obligation O and the date `at` of the change. 7I reads
O's established successors (`reach(O, obligation_replaces, as_object)`) and, for each successor,
its other established predecessors — the **one-hop transition group**: predecessors P (O and the
others a successor also replaced) and successors N. No successor → the B1 arm: the group is {O}
before and {O} after. Candidate and contested replacement claims are listed and take no part.

**The amounts — every one 7G's, with its provenance:**

```text
before   = Σ over predecessors p in P of   confirmed_p(at⁻)      p's position the day before the transition
after    = Σ over contexts c in P ∪ N of   confirmed_c(d)        where c's governing position is dated ≥ at
unaccounted(d) = after − before
```

`at⁻` is the day before `at`. A predecessor still open after the transition (B5: the card stays
open) is a context after as well as before. A context whose governing position at d is dated
*before* `at` is **not restated after the transition**: its 7G figure is a carry-forward of a
pre-transition statement, and counting it would let a replaced obligation's old balance survive by
silence — so that context is listed *not restated*, and `unaccounted` is **undetermined naming
it**. Undetermined propagates from every 7G term (no P-ack; a conflicted statement; a payment of
undetermined amount or direction — the third party's payoff, the insurer's payment). A negative
`unaccounted` is money that left the obligation with no disposition in the record (case 1's $900:
the insurer's $250 not attached to O, the $650 adjustment); a positive one is money that appeared
with no source (case 8's $300; a capitalised fee). Both are reported as they come.

**What 7I reads that is a disposition: nothing — and says so.** `dispositions: {read: [], note}`
until the vocabulary is ruled. The `unaccounted` amount is exactly what such claims would have to
explain; 7I names the contexts, the payments and the dates so the household knows what to assert.

**What it refuses:**

| Not this | Why |
|---|---|
| an amount on the relation — `obligation_replaces` read for scope, share or *$525 rolled in* | B5, B7; the relation carries none; its content is never read |
| the arrears as money — `overdue` subtracted, added or dispositioned | finding 3; 7G never reads an agreement-relative kind and neither does 7I |
| erasure — a replaced obligation's balance dropped to 0 because it was replaced | B15 (no ended / closed status derived); §1.5; *not restated* is the state |
| inference — the new lender's payoff relocated the balance; the insurer's payment reduced the bill | D′5, D′.11; a third party's payment has undetermined direction; the new context's own P-ack says where the money is |
| an anchoring — the check taking sides on M1 / M2 | the household chose B1 or B2; 7I reads what was asserted; case 8 is expressible as a gap |
| a disposition category — `waived`, `written_off`, `adjusted`, `capitalised` | the blocker; nothing is ruled |
| a chain — O3 replaces O1, O5 replaces O3 folded into one reconstruction | B11; one hop; two transitions |
| a container state — `partially_settled`, `closed` | finding 6; derived by the reader from the amounts |

### 7I.7 Rob's review, 2026-09-19 — the reconstruction made impossible to over-read; six amendments before ruling

Rob: *the central move is sound — while the vocabulary is blocked, the consumer can still perform
a reconstruction over already-established balances, respecting the disposition fence rather than
sneaking a category in through arithmetic. The issues are about making the reconstruction itself
impossible to over-read.* Six amendments; the first is a correction to §7I.2's formula, not a
clarification, and is recorded as one.

#### (1) The partition — `before_contexts` and `after_contexts`, explicit

§7I.2 wrote `after = Σ over contexts c in P ∪ N` and left "contexts" doing the work. Rob's hazard:
the same obligation appearing on both sides so that consolidation becomes `O1 + O2 + O4 − O1`
rather than `O4 − (O1 + O2)`. The sides, defined:

```text
before_contexts  = P   the established predecessor side of the single immediate transition
                       (O, and every other established direct predecessor of each established
                       direct successor of O)
after_contexts   = N ∪ P   the established resulting side — the successors, AND each predecessor
                       read AFTER the change: a predecessor is an after-context exactly when the
                       entitled party restated it at or after `at` (to its residual, or to 0)

before  = Σ_{p ∈ P} confirmed_p(at⁻)
after   = Σ_{n ∈ N} confirmed_n(d)  +  Σ_{p ∈ P, restated} confirmed_p(d)
```

**Each obligation contributes at most one term per side, and its two terms are different
figures at different dates** — `confirmed_p(at⁻)` on the before side, `confirmed_p(d)` on the
after side. The sides are disjoint *as (obligation, side) terms*, never `O1 − O1`. Why a
predecessor must be an after-context and not merely dropped: **erasure.** B5's card stays open
with $3,000 after O4 replaces $5,000 of it; a strictly disjoint `{O1} → {O4}` would report
`5,000 − 8,000 = −3,000 unaccounted` — money the record plainly still holds on O1. And in the
full replacement the predecessor's post-`at` restatement to $0 is *the* evidence that it went to
zero (Di3 vs Di4); without it the context is *not restated* and the check is undetermined. So:

```text
B4  consolidation   before = O1(at⁻) + O2(at⁻) + O3(at⁻) = 10,000
                    after  = O4(d) + O1(d) + O2(d) + O3(d) = 10,000 + 0 + 0 + 0     → 0
B5  partial         before = O1(at⁻) = 8,000
                    after  = O4(d) + O1(d) = 5,000 + 3,000                          → 0
B6  split           before = O1(at⁻) = 3,000
                    after  = O2(d) + O3(d) + O1(d) = 1,800 + 1,200 + 0              → 0
B1  same O          before = O(at⁻);  after = O(d)                                  (P = N = {O})
```

No family abstraction, no new concept: two derived sets and one rule for membership on each.

#### (2) One hop, mechanically

`N` = the established *direct* successors of O (`reach(O, obligation_replaces, as_object)`,
state established). `P` = {O} ∪ the established *direct* predecessors of each n ∈ N (`reach(n,
obligation_replaces)`, one call per successor, no recursion). Nothing is discovered two steps
away. Rob's diagram, as **Di20**:

```text
O1 ─► O2 ─► O3
O4 ───────► O3
anchored at O1:   N = {O2};  P = {O1} ∪ predecessors(O2) = {O1}.   O3 and O4 are not in the group.
anchored at O2:   N = {O3};  P = {O2, O4}.                          O1 is not in the group.
```

The transition being reconstructed has a single relation boundary; *other predecessors* are the
other direct predecessors of that immediate successor and nothing recursive. B11 preserved
without a family base.

#### (3) Eligibility for `after` is a post-transition P-ack, never a `confirmed(d)` value

The trap Rob names: 7G's `confirmed(d)` carries the governing position forward under G, so an old
$3,000 P-ack yields `confirmed(d) = 2,700` and *looks* restated. **Invariant:**

```text
A context is eligible for the after sum iff its governing stated position S(d) (7G: an
established balance P-ack by a party in effect as E) has as_of >= at.
confirmed(d) supplies the amount only after eligibility is established; a confirmed value
proves nothing about eligibility.
```

A context with `S(d).as_of < at` is *not restated*: listed, its carried-forward figure shown as
carried forward, and `unaccounted` undetermined naming it. A context with no S at all is likewise
not restated. Nothing about a `confirmed` value, its sign or its size makes a context eligible.

#### (4) `at⁻`, defined without a new primitive

`as_of` is a calendar date in the record. Every 7G predicate is `≤ d`. So **`confirmed(at⁻)` is
7G's `remaining(O, d = the calendar day before at)`** — the figure whose governing position and
qualifying events (statements, `occurred_on`, acknowledgments) are all dated strictly before `at`.
No instant, no special operation, no schema. **Events dated exactly `at` belong to the after
side**: a P-ack dated `at` is a restatement (eligible); a payment with `occurred_on = at` is not in
`before`; an acknowledgment dated `at` does not discharge for `before`. **Di21** tests each on the
boundary.

#### (5) `unaccounted` is arithmetic; "appeared" and "left" are withdrawn from the definition

```text
unaccounted(d) = after − before          and nothing else
```

The report carries `value`, `before`, `after` and their terms — **no sign label, no reading, no
key** for appeared / left / originated / waived. In prose, and only in prose: a positive gap is
*an amount present after that was not present before*; a negative gap is *an amount present
before that is not represented after*. Neither names a cause. `+300` is a reconstruction result,
not *$300 was originated*; `−900` is not *$900 was waived*. The consumer may say *this is the
slot a disposition claim would have to fill*; it never says what the claim is.

#### (6) The null model strengthened — negatives kept, zero is arithmetic

- **Di22 — the negative discriminator:** before 1,000, after 900 → `unaccounted = −100`, as it
  comes — not normalised, not absolute, not a status. 7G permits a negative `remaining`; 7I
  inherits *numbers are facts, not statuses*.
- **Di23 — zero is not a disposition:** a predecessor restated at $0 → `confirmed(d) = 0`, an
  arithmetic result of a P-ack and qualifying payments; the report carries no key and no word
  *discharged · closed · written_off · waived · disposition · none*; and the write-off shape —
  before 3,000, after 0 — is reported as `−3,000 unaccounted` and nothing more. **Zero means the
  entitled party stated zero; it does not mean gone.**
- Di18 stands, with its one-statement-moved-by-100 tripwire, and now sits between Di22 and Di23.

#### The boundary this pass must leave

```text
7I reconstructs established economic amounts across an immediate obligation transition.
It may identify an unaccounted difference.
It may not explain that difference by inventing a disposition, infer a disposition from zero,
infer a direction from the gap, collapse a chain, or choose between obligation outcome and
creditor accounting treatment.
```

**§7I.6 amended accordingly:** *"each context's confirmed position on the date asked, counting a
context only where the entitled party has restated it"* → *"the resulting side — the successors,
and each predecessor read after the change — counting a context only where its governing stated
position is dated at or after the change, eligibility established by that position and never by a
confirmed value; `at⁻` the calendar day before the change; `unaccounted = after − before`, a
number with its terms and no reading."* Cases Di20–Di23 added. **Ruling sought:** the partition
(1), one hop (2), the eligibility invariant (3), `at⁻` (4), the arithmetic definition (5), the
null-model discriminators (6), and the pass as amended.

## Pass 7H — Reconciliation

**The question, applied:** *Lakeview Health's EOB says my share of the ER visit is $412; Riverbend Hospital's statement
says I owe $450; Riverbend Hospital billed $1,300 — do these agree, and what is the $650 nobody has explained?*
The obligation-semantics question put the invariant before any of item 6 existed: *an unresolved
reconciliation balance must never be implicitly classified as paid, unpaid, reimbursed, deductible
or non-deductible* — the $650 *can be an unpaid balance, a payment the user made that has not been
recorded, an adjustment the provider wrote off, or a claim still being adjudicated; same number,
four meanings, three different tax answers.* It named the four determinations (`provider_adjustment
· user_paid_cash · unpaid · pending_claim`) as *current determinations superseded rather than
overwritten*, with `unpaid` and `pending_claim` yielding the same figure today and **not being the
same fact** (ARCHITECTURE_PRINCIPLES). C6 then closed the shortcut from the relation's side: *the
relation is not a reconciliation operator* — O's amount is not X's billed, allowed or patient
responsibility, nor a residual of them; *O's balance ≠ X's PR is not an error the substrate sees
and not a permission to compute a "corresponding obligation amount"; the four determinations are
item 7.* D10 and D12 said the same from the payment's side: *paid* is not a status the relation
confers; `unpaid` / `pending_claim` stay uncollapsed; a determination is *a reconciliation stance —
a consumer's reading over X, its amount claims, p and the relation; representable later as a dated
claim on X if item 7 rules it.* 7H is that consumer — and §3 named it, with 7J, as *the last place
a consumer could quietly become a resolver.*

So the pressure question: **what does reconciliation derive, if it may neither equate the two
sides nor determine anything?** Three things, each already licensed: it puts **the figures of two
different kinds of thing side by side with their provenance** — the provider's and insurer's
stances on X, the entitled party's position on O, the funding events in respect of X — through the
relations the household asserted; it states **the difference between them as a diagnostic**, the
way 7G states `statement_check` and 7I `unaccounted`; and it states **the unresolved amount** — the
obligation-semantics question's own arithmetic, *the gap between two different kinds of fact, an
observation about reconciliation, not an explanation of what happened to the money* — beside the
**determination the parties have made about it**, as dated stances, the current one named, none
inferred, none collapsed. And it shapes the determination as a claim, as D12 said item 7 would.

### 7H.2 What reconciliation is — the sides, the comparison, the unresolved amount, the determination

**The sides.** For one expense X on date d:

| side | what | source | governing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **X — the provider's stance** | `billed` (the charge) | the provider's dated claim on X (P-ack for X's charge), asserter in effect as *provider of X* at its date (a role kind — a stand-in until ruled) | G: the latest established at or before d, named; two at one date → conflict, none |
| **X — the insurer's stance** | `patient_responsibility` (the household's share); `plan_paid` (what the plan says it paid) | the insurer's dated claims on X, asserter in effect as *insurer of X* at its date | the same |
| **O — the entitled party's position** | `stated` S(d), `confirmed(d)` | 7G, for every O the household asserted *in respect of X* (`reach(X, obligation_in_respect_of, as_object)`, every claim, every state) | 7G's |
| **funding events** | every payment *in respect of X* (`reach(X, payment_in_respect_of, as_object)`), with amount, payer, payee, and any attachment to an O | the household's claims | — |

A claim on X by a party not in effect in the named role (Rob: *"the EOB said $412"* — F3 / K5) is
listed and does not govern — TG-base's rule on X's side.

**The comparison — one pair, one number, no verdict.** Where X has exactly one established
obligation in respect of it and that obligation is in respect of no other expense, 7H states
`difference = S_O(d) − PR_X(d)` with both terms named. Otherwise **undetermined, naming why**: no
obligation; several obligations (D′3 — no sum); the obligation in respect of several expenses (C5
— no apportionment); no governing PR; no governing S. The number is a diagnostic — *the two
positions differ by 38* — never *O should be 412*, never *matches* / *error*, never a corresponding
amount written anywhere.

**The unresolved amount — the question's own arithmetic, as an observation.**

```text
unresolved(d) = billed_X(d) − Σ dir·amt over payments in respect of X (relation established,
                                       amount established, b_billed < occurred_on ≤ d)
dir = −1 payee in effect as provider of X at the payment's date (a funding event — the insurer's,
          the household's, the HSA's: any payer);  +1 the provider is the payer (money back);
      undetermined otherwise → unresolved undetermined naming the payment
```

Payments dated at or before the governing charge's date are inside it (7G's rule on X's side).
This is the $650: *gross 1,200 − insurer 250 − user 300 = unresolved 650*. It is a number with its
terms. **It is classified by nothing 7H does.**

**The determination — a dated claim on X, shaped here as D12 said.** Kind `determination`
(catalogue), subject X, `as_of`, content one of the four names, asserted by a party under the
ordinary discipline; reading-sourced → candidate; retracted → contested. 7H reports the history
and the **current** determination (the latest established at or before d — *superseded, never
overwritten*; two at one date with different names → a conflict, none current). **It infers none:**
`unresolved = 0` is not `user_paid_cash`; no determination is not `unpaid`; a denial is not
`pending_claim`; `unpaid` and `pending_claim` are two names and stay two. The determination's
amount is the unresolved amount as the party found it — 7H attaches no split, no percentage.

### 7H.7 Rob's review of the shape, 2026-09-19 — four things settled in the record before any code

Rob, on the shape alone: *the overall split looks sound — dated positions from specific parties /
roles; the difference restricted to one-to-one; the unresolved amount without classifying the
remainder; determinations as claims and history, not inferred states. Four places need ruling
before implementation, so that code cannot make any of them accidentally canonical.* No build is
authorised; each is answered here from the record and the cases, with no new machinery.

#### (1) Obligation membership — the exact fence

*"Every O the household asserted in respect of X" must not become "all obligations reachable from
X" or "all that look related."* The proposition is exactly one: **`obligation_in_respect_of(O, X)`**
(item 6 pass C — subject the obligation, object the expense), read by `reach(X,
obligation_in_respect_of, as_object=True)` — every claim, every ground, each with its state — over
the **established referent of X** (7F's rule: an established `expense_corresponds` folds two
minted expenses into one referent; a candidate correspondence folds nothing). One hop, this kind
only: never through a payment, a plan, an artefact, a heading or a party.

```text
O is a reconciliation context of X   iff   some ground of some obligation_in_respect_of(O, X) is ESTABLISHED
                                           (utterance-sourced, unretracted)
candidate only  (reading-sourced grounds)   → listed under candidates; not a context; the side is NOT undetermined
contested only  (every ground retracted)    → listed under contested;  not a context; the side is NOT undetermined
established beside a retracted ground       → a context (the claim stands); the retracted ground listed
```

A candidate never unsettles (7C's rule; a matcher's pairing is a reason to ask); a withdrawn
assertion is the household's own withdrawal (R6 — it leaves the series, the history survives). The
**one-to-one condition** counts established contexts only, on both halves: exactly one established
context for X, and that context in respect of no *other* expense by an established claim
(`reach(O, obligation_in_respect_of)`, established endpoints ≠ the referent of X). Candidates and
contested on either half are named in the reason so their presence is visible, and break nothing.

#### (2) Funding-event membership — two predicates, kept apart

*"In respect of X" must not be inferred because a payment happens to go to the provider.* Two
independent predicates, in this order:

```text
(a) membership   p is a funding event of X   iff   some ground of some payment_in_respect_of(p, X) is ESTABLISHED
                 (7F reach over the referent of X; candidate-only / contested-only listed apart, not counted)
(b) direction    for a member p only, from p's own established paid_by / paid_to claims and 7F's role answer
                 at p's occurred_on:   payee in the referent of a party in effect as PROVIDER OF X  → −1 (funding)
                                        payer such a party                                          → +1 (money back)
                                        otherwise                                                   → undetermined,
                                                                                                      naming p
```

A payment to the provider with no established `payment_in_respect_of(p, X)` is **not in the report
at all** — not a candidate, not a funding event, nothing. *Any payer* is a statement about (b) —
the insurer's, the household's, the HSA's payment each counts as funding once (a) holds — never a
provider-account rule. §7H.2's sentence is corrected to read that way.

#### (3) The temporal anchor of the unresolved amount — and a correction to §7H.2

§7H.2 wrote `b_billed < occurred_on ≤ d` — 7G's base rule carried over. **That was wrong for a
charge.** 7G's base is a *balance*, which absorbs the payments before it; a `billed` position is
the **gross charge**, which absorbs nothing — a corrected charge re-stated on a later date is still
gross. So:

```text
B(d)          = the governing billed position: the latest ESTABLISHED billed claim on X with as_of <= d
                by a party in effect as provider of X at its as_of (G, named with its date; two at one
                date with different values → conflict, none; none → no charge)
unresolved(d) = B(d).value − Σ dir(p)·amt(p)   over every funding event p of X (predicates (a) and (b))
                                                 with ESTABLISHED amount and occurred_on <= d — no lower bound
```

The economic date is `occurred_on`, always; no acknowledgment, confirmation or `created_at`
substitutes for it (7G's discipline). **All established funding events count, irrespective of any
acknowledgment or discharge** — explicitly: the two sides are different kinds (the household's
record of money moved, D; the provider's acknowledgment lives on O, 7G) and 7H sets them beside
each other rather than merging them: `unresolved` is the funding view, and each context's 7G
`confirmed(d)` beside it is the acknowledged view. Undetermined propagates from B (absent,
conflicted) and from any member's amount, direction or date.

#### (4) The determination — what makes a claim eligible, and what "current" is

**The claim.** Kind `determination` (catalogue); subject X; `as_of` required — the date the
determination is about; content **exactly one of the four names** (`provider_adjustment ·
user_paid_cash · unpaid · pending_claim`); any other content is listed as *unrecognised* and is
never current (C7's rule: one of these *or not at all* — a denial forced in would acquire a
channel's meaning). Established = utterance-sourced and unretracted; reading-sourced → candidate
(a parser determines nothing); retracted → contested.

**Who may make it.** Any party under the ordinary discipline — the household (D12), the provider,
the insurer. **Whether a party's role constrains its determination is the catalogue's to rule, not
7H's**: the asserter and 7F's role answers for it (provider of X · insurer of X · a household
person) are *reported as facts* and constrain nothing; when the catalogue rules who may determine,
the eligibility predicate gains that test exactly as TG-base gained the entitled-party test, with no
change of shape.

**"Current" — no new resolver.** The only ordering machinery the record has ruled is **G, per
series** (TS: a stance stands until the next stance, for one party, one kind, one identity) and
**conflict at one date → none** (7G). 7H uses those and nothing else:

```text
for each asserting party A:   current_A(d) = the latest established determination on X by A with as_of <= d
                              two by A at that date with different names        → A's series conflicted, no current_A
                              the latest by A retracted                         → it leaves A's series; the previous one is current_A
                              (R6: the history that it was said survives, listed as contested)
current(d):   every party's current_A agrees      → that name, with every asserter named
              the parties' currents differ        → CONFLICT — all listed, none current
              no party has a current              → none
```

*Superseded, never overwritten* is therefore **within one party's series** — Rob says
`pending_claim` in March and `provider_adjustment` in May: the March claim is listed, the May one is
current_Rob. **A later claim by a different party supersedes nothing**: Riverbend Hospital's `provider_adjustment`
beside Rob's `user_paid_cash` is a conflict between parties, reported, unresolved — recency across
parties would be a resolver 7H does not have. Two contemporaneous claims by different parties that
agree are one current determination with two asserters.

**Nothing infers a determination**, restated with the four items in view: `unresolved = 0` is
arithmetic; no claim is no determination; a denial is neither name; the two names are two facts.

### 7H.8 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-19: the 7H opening shape RULED on the four items; no build authorised

## Pass 7J — Accumulators, eligibility, overlap

**The question, applied:** *have we met the deductible? — who is on which plan today? — Kim is
on the work plan and my marketplace plan, so which one?* The last consumer, and the one §3 put
last because it is where a household-level answer is most tempting to manufacture from several
legitimate lower-level facts. Each of its three words already has a ruling that narrows it:

- **Accumulators.** Pl10: *deductible met $1,800 of $3,000 as of 3/1* is **the insurer's dated
  stance about P** (or about Kim under P); *a consumer may derive the same figure from
  adjudications; disagreement is a derived dispute, never a stored status.* Pl15: *terms never
  entered* is the **absence of term claims**, not `deductible = 0`. APPLICABILITY: applicability
  is a fact about the rule, determinability a fact about our knowledge; **they are orthogonal,
  and absence must not acquire the semantics of the representation through which it passes** —
  *your plan has no deductible* versus *we don't have your deductible yet* versus *this visit
  doesn't count*; and the one sentence that must never be permitted: *you have met your
  deductible* for a deductible nobody entered.
- **Eligibility.** §F.2 / F5: *under a plan* is **membership as the household asserts it**, an
  evidence boundary, not objective eligibility; *eligible · enrolled · terminated* are **the
  insurer's dated stances** — content, propositions — and *Lakeview Health's "never eligible" leaves the
  household's "Kim is under P" standing as a claim.* Coverage ③ (whether services are covered) is
  never derived from membership.
- **Overlap.** F20 / R7: two plans, two claims, **no selector**, no *primary*; overlap is
  detectable, never resolved. The concurrent-coverage ruling: *period overlap does not determine
  applicability; applicability is evaluated for the particular expense; where two plans genuinely
  govern, concurrency is a determinate multi-valued state, not unresolvedness* — and priority,
  where unresolved, is an unresolved fact **within** that state. E24: *person → plan + person →
  expense ⇒ expense → plan* is convergence, refused; which plan an expense is in respect of is
  the household's asserted relation (E1, unbuilt).

each narrow. *Who is under what on d* is 7F's `in_effect` over the membership role — 7J adds only

### 7J.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-19: 7J RULED as written; no code authorised by this ruling

**1. `plans_in_effect` is a fact report, not a coverage resolver.** The derivation is exactly:
*which plans are established as in effect for the person on d, under F's membership machinery.*
Multiple established plans produce multiple plans in effect — an overlap fact; no `primary`, no
selector, no precedence rule, no attempt to decide which plan "really" covers the person. The
insurer's `eligible` / `terminated` positions are a separate dated-claim surface: they do not
override the household's membership position and do not themselves create or remove membership;
candidate material remains candidate material. **Pl12's retroactive letter cannot silently become
a termination merely because the insurer asserts it.**

**2. `accumulator` has three deliberately independent axes.** The term is its own proposition —
numeric → the rule exists with that amount; `none` → the rule does not exist; `unknown` → no
established claim of the rule exists — three states kept distinct: `none` is not zero and
`unknown` is not `none`. The stated accumulator is the insurer's established stance, governed and
named under G / Pl10, **not replaced by the derived calculation**. The derived accumulator is a
constrained sum: within the governing plan-year window; only the insurer's own established
`applied_to_deductible` stances; only for expenses the household has asserted in respect of P;
each plan deriving only its own share; **no membership-to-application inference** — E's stand-in
relation read as the applicable asserted fact, not reconstructed from the fact that someone
belongs to a plan.

**3. Exempt and unadjudicated expenses remain visible**, never silently folded into arithmetic:
an exempt expense is excluded from the sum and listed as exempt; an expense lacking the required
adjudication is neither treated as zero nor ignored — it makes the derived figure undetermined
with the expense named — preserving *this expense contributes zero* versus *we cannot establish
whether this expense contributes*.

**4. `dispute` and `remaining` are consequences, not new determinations.** Where both sides are
numerically established, `dispute = stated − derived` and `remaining = term − derived`, the latter
permitted to be negative when the derived amount exceeds the term. Neither becomes a stored fact,
verdict, eligibility determination or Boolean. **There is no `met` key**; a deductible whose term is
`unknown` or `none` cannot acquire a fabricated *met* state merely because an arithmetic path
happens to produce zero.

**5. Concurrent plans stay concurrent.** CC1's rule is the fence: each plan knows its own share.
7J must not sum multiple plans into a single household accumulator, select one plan, or apportion
an expense merely because several plans are in effect. Pl2's family / individual distinction
remains whatever the applicable plan's established term says; 7J invents no household-level
governing figure.

**6. No Boolean eligibility / coverage verdict.** 7J does not answer *is this person covered? ·
which plan covers this expense? · is the deductible met? · which insurer governs? · is the
household's figure correct?* It reports the established positions and derives only the explicitly
ruled arithmetic from them.

**Boundary.** The two consumers are properly distinct — `plans_in_effect` → established membership
positions in effect on d, including overlap; `accumulator` → term + stated position + constrained
derived accumulation, with dispute / remaining as arithmetic consequences. Nothing in 7J resolves
the overlap F reports; nothing converts insurer eligibility assertions into membership. Ju1–Ju19
are the right candidate boundary, including the null model and the failure loci. **No additional
resolver, Boolean, household authority, `met` state or persistence mechanism.** **7J ruled as
written. No code is authorised by this ruling; stopped until the separate 7J build authorisation.**
