## 1. The discipline — what a consumer is, and twelve things it may never do

**A consumer is a read-only derivation over the record that answers one question, returns its
answer with the claims it rests on, and reports what it could not settle.** It is not a resolver,
not a status, not a learner, not a second ontology.

1. **A consumer never writes.** No derived status, resolved referent, chosen value, effective
   period, ranking or "current" anything is persisted — the no-silent-learning boundary: an
   unsurfaced observation may never persist.
2. **A consumer reports ambiguity; it never resolves it.** Contested stays contested; a dispute
   is *derived and shown*, never settled by recency, position, count or source type (`[-1]` is
   the audit's R4); overlap is detected, never resolved (R7 / F7 / F20); "nothing chosen" is a
   valid and often the correct answer.
3. **A consumer traverses established relations; it never composes across kinds** (the D′.11
   constraint) and never manufactures a relation from convergence through a shared person,
   identity, date or document (§D′.2, E24, F13 / F14).
4. **A consumer reads the endpoint it reaches; it never projects that endpoint's properties onto
   the subject** (C17 / C21): reaching X from O lets it read X's nature claims *as X's*, by whom;
   it does not make O medical.
5. **Absence is not zero.** A consumer tests completeness before judging (the $7,053 shortfall
   that was a surplus): *terms never entered* ≠ `deductible = 0` (APPLICABILITY); an absent
   relation ≠ *unpaid* (D6); no `under_plan` claims ≠ *no members* (F12); `Unknown` is a
   terminal state, not a default.
6. **A consumer's answer carries its provenance** — which claims, by which parties, contested or
   not — and the narrator may voice only what is asserted in the findings record (constraint 3):
   a consumer's *derivation* is never itself an assertion.
7. **A consumer never ranks by type, channel or count.** Independence is between origins, per
   proposition, three-valued (C4); readings of one referent never corroborate (the one-source
   rule, Pc2 / Pc9); a process contributes nothing (O9).
8. **A consumer is one consumer.** It computes one question; there is no master status and no
   master label (§7.7's *three facts consumed by different behaviours — not an ontology*).
   Where two consumers disagree, that disagreement is reportable and is not a third consumer's to
   settle.
9. **A consumer names where its failure can appear and is measured there** — downstream of no
   guard that would absorb the evidence.
10. **A consumer's threshold is null-modelled before it ships** — twice the obvious rule was a
    coin flip.
11. **No consumer may become a resolver that other consumers read as fact** (Q2's resolver,
    fenced since slice 2): a consumer may take another consumer's *output with its provenance* as
    input; it may never take it as a claim.
12. **A wiring hazard is repaired by the consumer that arrives at it**, in the mode the audit
    named, and the repair is a refusal where the audit said refusal (R4: a distinct amount →
    refuse, never `attested[-1]`).

**The stopping rule for this item:** *a derivation that cannot state which claims it read, which it
could not settle, and where its own failure would show, is not a consumer — it is a status with a
function signature.*

### 7A.6 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7A RULED as proposed; the build authorised after one wording guard

*The central ruling is correct: a parse dispute is a read-only consumer derivation over
observations of one artefact / referent; it reports disagreement; it does not resolve
disagreement.* The strongest part: `disputed` is not made into ontology — the dispute is a
question answered from the record, not a property attached to the proposition. Against the
governing question — *what may it say?* the observations disagree, and precisely how; *on whose
authority?* the readings, propositions, correspondence state and their provenance; *what may it
not turn into fact?* a selected value, kind, established correspondence or "current"
interpretation.

**The cases, as Rob read them:** Pd1 vs Pd2 give the two states needed — *a candidate that
these are the same line, and if it holds they disagree* vs *established as the same line, and
they disagree* — without a dispute state in the database, and Pd1 must never upgrade candidate →
established. Pd3 / Pd8 are the anti-majority cases: three observations, two contents, one source
— the two `$44,000`s do not outvote the `$4,400`; **multiplicity of observation must not
masquerade as evidentiary independence.** Pd5's omission is a classification, not a dispute —
absence is not zero. Pd6 / Pd7 keep 7A from becoming a general-purpose conflict engine: one
artefact, multiple readings — not artefact ↔ artefact, not assertion ↔ reading. Pd9: a
human-produced reading remains a reading; the reader's identity does not smuggle semantic weight
back in. Pd10: a retracted correspondence is *contested*, the consumer cannot settle whether these
are one line, and it stops — *unresolved substrate state is an answer boundary, not an invitation
to guess.*

**The wording guard, made explicit before coding.** *"Groups of propositions that are one line"*
risked sounding as if the consumer knew line identity. It does not:

```text
established correspondence  →  established referent / component   (referent())
candidate correspondence    →  candidate grouping only
no correspondence           →  no claim that the propositions are one referent
```

7A reports what follows from each state; it is **never a second correspondence mechanism** —
exactly the wording that later becomes an innocent `[0]` or helper.

**The implementation boundary, protected aggressively:** `parse_disputes` **must not call
anything whose purpose is to establish correspondence.** It consumes established correspondence,
candidate correspondence and `referent()`; it may never do *"these look like the same line" →
create correspondence*. Candidates originate from the already-fenced matcher / reading machinery;
7A merely reports them.

**The `resolution()` hazard is correctly assigned to 7A** — the old failure *read history →
latest value → call it resolved*; 7A gives *readings → correspondence state → disagreement →
provenance → no choice*, and `resolution()` may not read past that layer. A wiring repair, not a
new concept, as the audit classified it — and H4's boundary stands: **no resolver change**; the
reading-level answer lives in the 2.0 substrate where the referent now exists.

**The null model is a correctness oracle, not a statistical measure:** same established referent +
same kind / content = no dispute; same referent + different content / kind = dispute; identical
re-parse → 0, one changed figure → 1; **no `if similarity > threshold` — ever.**

**Disposition — order ACCEPTED; 7A RULED; implementation authorised exactly as narrow as the
record proposes:** one read-only consumer, no schema, no persistence, no threshold, no resolver,
and a fixture / smoke suite whose mutations specifically attempt to make it choose. **7B is not
opened until 7A's build is green.**

## Pass 7B — Independence / the one-source rule

**Rob's question, verbatim:** *when two claims appear to support the same proposition, what
establishes that their origins are independent, and what observations are insufficient to
establish independence?* Opened as a semantic pressure test, **cases first, no `is_independent()`
designed** — 7A has already established *three readings → one referent → one source*, and 7B
attacks the next shortcut:

```text
different reading · different parser · different document · different channel
· different party · different timestamp · different row
        ↓
therefore independent            — none of these follows from difference
```

Two things made explicit before the cases, on Rob's instruction: **independence is not a
strength score** — 7B may say *independent · not independent · undetermined* and may never turn
that into *two independent sources = stronger* (that would smuggle 7E into 7B); and **"independent"
cannot mean "different database rows"** — the whole provenance work exists because two rows can
descend from one origin.

### 7B.1 What is already fixed

| Ruling | Constraint on 7B |

### 7B.2 The shortcut, interrogated — what "different" would smuggle

| "Different …" | Why it does not establish independence | The case |
|---|---|---|
| **reading** | two readings of one artefact are two observations of one thing (7A) | In1 |
| **parser / process** | a process contributes nothing; a re-parse by a newer parser is a reading | In1, In11 |
| **document / artefact** | two artefacts from one origin are one origin (two EOBs from Lakeview Health); distinct artefacts are *necessary for some* independence, never sufficient | In3 |
| **channel** | the channel is how the reading reached Groundwork, never who knows (K5: the user reporting the EOB is the insurer's origin through the user's channel) | In5 |

| **timestamp / row** | recency and row identity are nothing; the same utterance evidenced twice is one origin | In2 |
| **correspondence** | two artefacts or two parties established as corresponding are one; a *candidate* correspondence leaves independence **undetermined**, not false | In7 |

What establishes independence is exactly C4's sentence and nothing weaker: **distinct origins,
each knowing for the proposition, each chain to its origin established** — where *origin* is read
from the assertion's provenance (C1), *knowing* from `derived_strength` (per relationship), and
*chain* from asserted links (C3) and correspondence (one referent = one origin). Everything else
is *not independent* (same origin) or *undetermined* (a chain or an origin not established).

### 7B.6 Ruling candidate — pass 7B, for Rob

> **Independence is a three-valued derivation between origins, for one proposition, and
> nothing weaker or stronger. Two claims are independent exactly when they resolve to two
> distinct established origins, each knowing for the proposition, each with its chain
> established — the origin read from the assertion's provenance, never from who is speaking, the
> chain read from asserted links and established correspondence, the knowing read per
> relationship. They are not independent when they resolve to one origin — through one
> artefact, through two artefacts of one origin, through two channels of one source, through a
> duplicated utterance, through two mediated chains to one entitled party, or through a
> correspondence a party established. And independence is undetermined — never defaulted to
> either answer — when a chain or an origin is a candidate, contested or unasserted. Difference
> establishes nothing: not a different reading, parser, document, channel, party, timestamp or
> row. A process is not an origin and contributes nothing; a candidate is not a claim by anyone.
> The verdict is a set of origins and their pairwise states, never a count, a rank, a score or a
> strength — three independent origins are three independent pairs and no number; what a
> consumer may make of independent origins is 7E's question and is not answered here.
> Independence is never a property of a document, a source or a party: the same two artefacts
> are one origin for one proposition and, for another, one knowing party and one that is not.
> Reader independence for what a document says remains fenced. Nothing is stored; nothing is
> resolved; the 1.x count of distinct source types is named as what it is — a count of channel
> types — and is not repaired by counting differently.**

**If ruled, the build is one read-only function** — `independence(session, proposition)` beside
`parse_disputes`, plain structures; a fixture over In1–In18 with the absences (no `n_sources` /

**What pass 7B leaves exactly where it found it:** about-ness through correspondence (7C) ·
level consistency (7D) · strength and weakest-link (7E) · RA5 reader independence · candidate

*The line: two claims are independent when two different parties each knew, and each said so in

### 7B.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7B ACCEPTED / SEMANTICALLY CLOSED; implementation within §7B.6 only

*7B did not invent an "independence detector". It established what independence means in this
ontology and, as importantly, what observations cannot establish it.*

**Why accepted.** In5 / In5′ expose the trap: *different people ≠ different origins* — provenance
determines origin, speaker identity does not; and the same speaker does not collapse genuinely
distinct knowing into one origin if provenance establishes otherwise. In12 closes the subtler
loophole: independence cannot attach to an artefact, document, party or source as a reusable
property; it is evaluated for the proposition being considered. The semantic unit is

```text
proposition → assertion → provenance → origin → established chain + knowing
```

not *document / source / person → independence status*.

**The three-valued result is essential.** The candidate-correspondence case is the important one:
two origins that *might* be connected, with only a candidate correspondence, have earned neither
conclusion — *not independent* would manufacture a negative fact, *independent* a positive one;
**undetermined is the truthful terminal state**, consistent with the rule that unresolved evidence
stays unresolved rather than being normalised into a usable status.

**7B correctly stops before 7E.** In14 is the fence: three independently established origins
yield *three origins, three independent pairs* and nothing about evidentiary strength — no
*corroborated · stronger · confidence · majority · score · threshold · preferred source*.
Otherwise 7B becomes the first half of a strength engine and 7E a formatter. **7B: are these
origins independent? 7E: what may be said about the evidentiary significance of multiple links?**
Different questions.

**In15 preserved as a regression target.** The old `distinct = {e.source}` is not "a bad
implementation of independence"; it answers a different question — how many source / channel
identities occur in the record. The audit language is preserved rather than "fixed" into a more
sophisticated count, which could still be completely wrong semantically.

**The 7B wiring guard, made explicit before implementation:** *the consumer may inspect the
established provenance / relationship chain to determine the three-valued result, but it may not
establish, upgrade, resolve or mutate any provenance, correspondence, knowing relationship or
independence relationship while doing so.*

```text
established record → 7B reads provenance → follows the established chain
→ evaluates knowing for this proposition → independent / not independent / undetermined

never:   7B sees evidence → creates correspondence → creates provenance → declares independence
```

**Independence remains a consumer answer, not a new ontology relation.**

**Established, as Rob recorded it:** (1) per proposition · (2) origin from assertion provenance,
not speaker identity · (3) distinct origins required · (4) each knowing for that proposition · (5)
the chain established · (6) different rows, readings, parsers, documents, channels, parties or
timestamps do not establish independence · (7) candidate or unresolved chains → undetermined,
never guessed · (8) process observations do not become independent knowing by being separate
records · (9) not strength; no count / rank / score · (10) the consumer writes and creates none
of the relationships it evaluates · (11) failure reported at the claim where evidence cannot
establish the result · (12) the null model is the correctness oracle.

**Disposition — 7B: ACCEPTED / SEMANTICALLY CLOSED.** 7C stays unopened until 7B is implemented
and its semantic tests pass. **The natural next question for 7C is not "how do we calculate
about-ness?" but *what exactly establishes that a proposition is about an artefact when
correspondence is involved, and what tempting paths must explicitly fail?***

### 7C.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7C RULED as written; four semantic edges settled before the build

*The framing needed no reframing. Narrower issues than 7B's, but one important enough not to pass
as wording.* All nine points settled here, in §7C.2 and in the cases Ac21–Ac28.

1. **"Nothing contradicts" is a defined condition, not a hidden resolver.** *Artefact-level
   derivation is available only when the artefact's established concern set for the requested
   identity type contains exactly one identity and no established contrary concern for that same
   type. Candidate, contested, unestablished or otherwise unresolved concerns do not satisfy the
   condition and are not resolved by this consumer.* The one established concern supplies the
   derivation; the absence of a second identity is not evidence that the first is true (Ac22).
2. **"Exactly one" is scoped to the requested identity type.** An artefact concerning O1, E1 and
   P1 is about O1 for the obligation type — E1 and P1 are neither multiplicity nor conflict; an
   artefact concerning O1 and O2 is ambiguous for that type however settled everything else is
   (Ac21).
3. **Correspondence does the work only as established identity of recordings.** The contract is
   mechanical: *P is about X through a chain only if every traversed correspondence link is
   established*; a candidate link stops the chain there — the mixed chain *P ↔ Q established, Q ↔
   R candidate, R → X* yields X as a candidate for P, never derived (Ac23).
4. **Cycles and multiple paths create no multiplicity.** *P ↔ Q, Q ↔ R, R ↔ P, R → X* is one
   component and one derivation (Ac24); *P → artefact A → X* and *P → Q → artefact B → X* is one
   derived entry with two paths listed (Ac25). **Multiple established derivations terminating in
   the same identity do not strengthen, rank or multiply the about-ness result** — 7B's
   multiplicity fence in 7C's clothing; paths are not counted.
5. **Provenance labels are not an ordering of authority.** *Structural / asserted / derived
   describe how an about-ness result is obtained; where independently established paths yield
   incompatible identities of one type, 7C reports the conflict and does not resolve it* — 7D's
   territory, kept out of 7C (Ac14, Ac26).
6. **"Own established participant" is precise.** Structural about-ness is Pa10's relationship —
   an established participant slot of one of the **identity types that about-ness ranges over**
   (obligation · payment · expense · plan, the concerns kinds' object types). A party subject is
   not "about the party" in this sense; `if proposition.subject_party_id: return about(...)` is
   exactly the silent expansion refused (Ac27).
7. **A reading is a carrier of access, not an about-ness edge.** *A reading is a carrier of access
   to its artefact's established semantics; it is not itself an about-ness assertion and cannot
   create or transmit a concerns relation merely because a read proposition says so.* *Reading →
   artefact → artefact concerns X* can work; *reading → proposition that concerns X* cannot, and a
   sibling proposition's asserted concerns transmits nothing to P (Ac28).
8. **Established and candidate paths coexisting — ruled, not chosen by implementation:** the
   established path reaches; the candidate is **reported as a candidate beside it** — faithful to
   the evidence architecture; and a candidate can never turn an otherwise settled result into
   *undetermined*, which would make candidate evidence a resolver (Ac26).
9. The two rejections Rob singled out stand as architectural boundaries: *Harbor is E of O1 ⇒ its
   lines are about O1* — the role → about-ness leak with no valid general rule (Ac13); *P about O,
   O in respect of X ⇒ P about X* — D′.11 stays a separate question, not an accidental composition
   operator in 7C (Ac12).

```text
                    ABOUT IDENTITY
                          │
          ┌───────────────┼────────────────┐
      structural       asserted        derived
                                      /          \
                                  artefact    correspondence

candidate ──→ does not reach      contested ──→ stops       multiple identities ──→ conflict, no choosing
composition ─→ does not follow    convergence ─→ does not infer   reading itself ──→ does not establish
derivation ──→ does not write     derivation ──→ does not know
```

**The question, applied:** the credit-union statement is asserted to concern the mortgage and the
HELOC; line 3 is asserted to concern the car loan. *Do the artefact-level and the proposition-level
about-ness claims agree — and when they do not, what may a consumer say?* Pa7 answered the last

guard's to refuse and never a resolver's to settle.* Ab7's findings left the report itself
unbuilt. 7C now reports the conflict *as found* on one proposition; **7D characterises it** —
from the artefact's side, across its lines, by kind, with every asserter — and stops.

7D is the pass Rob named as the one where 7C must not have quietly acquired conflict-resolution
authority. So the first thing 7D does is say what it is not: **it is not a resolver, and the
report it produces is not a verdict.** A consumer that reports *the artefact-level claim by Rob is
contradicted by three line-level claims by Kim* has stated a fact about the record; a consumer
that concludes *so the artefact-level claim is wrong* has manufactured one.

### 7D.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-16: 7D RULED as written; six things the fixture must demonstrate

*Correctly framed; the crucial distinction preserved — 7D is a characterisation / reporting consumer,
not a resolver.* The core definition is right: **an established line-level about-ness identity
outside the artefact's established identity set for the same requested type** — level consistency,
not general proposition disagreement; the negative cases keep 7D from becoming a generic
contradiction detector.

**The edges settled, and the six demonstrations required before acceptance:**

1. **Empty artefact-level evidence is neither consistency nor inconsistency.** The three-state
   distinction is mechanical, never collapsed by `if line in artefact_identities … else
   inconsistent`: *{O1} / O1 → consistent · {O1} / O2 → inconsistent · {} / O1 → no level basis.*
2. **Contested artefact evidence has exact STOP semantics.** *O1 established, O2 contested; P → O1,
   Q → O2*: the report preserves *O1 → established basis exists; O2 → basis contested*, derives no
   level disagreement for Q from the contested state, and does not pretend the artefact's set is
   simply {O1} in order to manufacture one — a line outside the established set while the
   artefact side is contested for that type is **unresolved**, not inconsistent; a line inside it
   is consistent (its basis is established regardless).
3. **Candidate paths never become inconsistencies.** *7C candidate → 7D sees candidate → NOT a
   level inconsistency* — a line whose only about-ness is candidate has **no line basis**; a
   candidate artefact concern is not part of the established set. Otherwise 7D would acquire
   exactly the promotion authority 7C was forbidden.
4. **Type isolation applies at the report level.** The report is partitioned *artefact → identity
   type → {artefact established set · lines by classification}*; an artefact concerning O1, E1 and
   p never makes an obligation line about O1 "in conflict" because of E1 or p (Ac21's rule).
5. **No Boolean.** The report distinguishes **consistent · inconsistent · no line basis · no level
   basis · unresolved** — never `consistent=False` for the last three. And artefact-derived
   about-ness (7C's artefact path) is *the artefact level itself* and is excluded from the
   comparison — a line whose only established about-ness is artefact-derived has no independent
   line basis; correspondence-derived about-ness does count (Lc-g).
6. **Counts are descriptive metadata about the observed report, not an evidentiary primitive.**
   *1 / 5 · 3 / 5 · 5 / 5 inconsistent* all remain reportable without 7D acquiring majority,
   confidence, weight, probability or a recommended retraction.

**The unit and the dependency direction, pinned:** `aboutness()` → established / candidate /
undetermined / conflict → *7D interprets only the established about-ness, with its provenance,
never as a claim* → the report. Never *7D reinterprets participants → creates about-ness*. 7D is
a consumer of 7C's result, not a second implementation of about-ness. The assertion-disagreement
class (Lc-h) stays separate: *proposition ↔ proposition* versus *line ↔ artefact* are different
questions.

**Disposition — 7D: RULED as written; build it.** 7D characterises the disagreement; which

### 7E.2 What a link is — the chain vocabulary, closed

A **chain** is one evidentiary relationship of the value followed by the links of one 7C path.
Every link is **a claim on record with its own evidentiary relationships**, or the value's own
evidence; nothing else is a link. The vocabulary, today:

| Link | What it is | Its strength |
|---|---|---|
| **L0 — the value's evidence** | one evidentiary relationship of P (an utterance, or a reading whose artefact's origin is derived inside slice 3) | `derived_strength(rel, knowing)` — **as given**; the origin link and O5's laundering test live here |
| **`proposition_concerns(P, X)`** | the asserted about-ness (7C *asserted*) | `derived_strength` of its evidentiary relationship(s): the minter-of-X relation — *knowing* for the household member who declared X, *not_knowing* for another asserter |
| **`artefact_concerns(A, X)`** | the artefact-level claim (7C *derived: artefact*, where A's established set for the type is exactly {X}) | the same relation; the reading between P and A is a carrier of access, not a link — it has no strength of its own and adds none |
| **`proposition_corresponds(P, P′)`** | an established correspondence (7C *derived: correspondence*) followed by P′'s structural or asserted about-ness | **undetermined by design** (Kn4) — until correspondence has a ruled epistemology, every chain through it is undetermined |
| **structural** | P's own established participant (7C *structural*) | not a link: the chain is L0 alone |

**Not links:** a reading or a parser (Kn9 — channels; no party, no strength) · a role claim (no

candidate origin — there is no chain, so there is no strength) · a contested anything (7C stops;
so does 7E) · the *count* of paths, origins, readings or asserters.

**The operation:** *within a link*, the link's strength is the strength class of its evidence —
where one claim has several established evidentiary relationships, the strongest of them, which
is slice 3's own precedent for parallel evidence of one claim and is not a ranking of parties
(every ground is listed with its class; the link's class answers *is there first-hand evidence
for this link on record*); *along a chain*, the weakest link, by the substrate's `_weakest`;
*across chains to one identity*, **nothing** — each chain is listed with its class, none is
chosen, none is summed, none is counted. *Across identities*, nothing — a conflict stays a
conflict.

### 7E.7 Ruling — Rob, 2026-09-17: 7E ACCEPTED as proposed, with one tightening before implementation

**Ground aggregation belongs entirely to `derived_strength`.** *7E must inherit the substrate's
existing ground resolution literally rather than introduce its own notion of "strongest."* Ms23
is accepted **by inheritance, not redesigned**: for each link, 7E consumes the class already
established by that link's substrate derivation — never *one link → 7E invents its own ground
aggregation → a different link class*. Two reasons: a chain with zero extra links must be
identical to `derived_strength`; and 7E cannot introduce a second interpretation of parallel
evidence merely because it is now looking at that evidence as part of a chain. **7E does not
define ground aggregation.**

**"Weakest" operates only on links.** Ms2 as written: `L0 = knowing · concerns = not_knowing →
chain = not_knowing`, and the value's L0 remains knowing — 7E is not saying *the value is not
known*; it is saying *the derivation of this proposition's about-ness through this chain has
class not_knowing* — exactly the accidental leakage Ab7 prevents. Likewise `L0 = knowing ·
correspondence = undetermined → chain = undetermined`: an established correspondence-derived
about-ness does not acquire epistemic strength because its endpoints happen to be strong.

**The multi-chain rule accepted explicitly:** `O1 ├ chain A → knowing ├ chain B → not_knowing
└ chain C → undetermined` as three independently reported chains — not `O1 → not_knowing`, not
`O1 → knowing`, not `O1 → 3 pieces of evidence`, and certainly not `O1 → 2/3 knowing → stronger`.
**7E does not need — and must not acquire — a per-identity strength result.** Ms7's STOP: a
candidate correspondence between two strong ends → *no chain → no chain class*; two strong
endpoints cannot bridge an unestablished relation. Ms20/Ms21 exactly as written: `no evidence ≠
undetermined · no chain ≠ undetermined` — *undetermined* is the class of something evaluated
that could not be established at the required level; absence is not an epistemic result.

**Two implementation-sensitive points the ruling makes explicit (required before code):**

1. **Ground aggregation belongs entirely to `derived_strength`.** 7E consumes; it never
   re-derives or re-aggregates.
2. **`unchained` and `conflicts` are report conditions, not strength classes.** *Conflict is
   reported as an existing structural condition; 7E assigns no strength class to the conflict
   itself.* *Unchained* does not mean *strength = undetermined*; it means there is no established
   chain on which chain strength can operate.

**The acceptance fence — what 7E must not do:** recompute `derived_strength` · aggregate grounds
independently of `derived_strength` · rank parties / origins · read 7B independence · resolve 7C

identities · convert absence into `undetermined` · convert candidate correspondence into a chain ·
convert contested / candidate relations into established links · grade the value itself · write
strength back anywhere · create a new strength lattice. **7E's only new operation is composition:
consume the class of each already-established link and take the weakest class along one
established chain. That is the entire derivation.**

**The 7E invariant:**

```text

already-established substrate class and taking the weakest class along that chain;
```

### 7E.8 The seam Rob named — what a chain *is*, 2026-09-18

Rob's question, before 7F may interpret it for itself: *what prevents 7E from enumerating the same
semantic chain more than once through structurally equivalent traversals — are `A → B → C` and
`A → B → C`, reached by two distinct structural traversals, one reported chain or two?* And: does
"simple" have an exact operational meaning?

**What 7C says.** 7C's unit is the *path*: one per correspondent proposition and about-ness
(`(proposition_corresponds, P, P′) · structural P′` or `· proposition_concerns(claim, asserter)`);
*several established paths to one identity are one derivation with its paths listed; two routes are
two explanations of one derived about-ness, not two pieces of evidence; a cycle is one component
and one derivation.* 7C does not expand routes into hops and does not identify paths by grounds —
it lists one path per `(claim, ground)` of the correspondent's assertion, because `_about_direct`
reports asserted about-ness per evidentiary relationship. **So 7C's path identity does not answer
7E's question**; 7E's unit is finer, and the record must say what it is.

**Ruling applied — a chain is its sequence of grounds.** `(L0, ground₁ … groundₙ)`, each an
evidentiary-relationship id. Two enumerations that reach the same sequence are **one chain, reported
once**, however many structural traversals reach it. Two different claims on record between the same
nodes — a second `proposition_corresponds` between one pair, a second `proposition_concerns` naming
one identity — are two grounds, hence **two chains**, however alike: a link is a claim on record and
two claims are two. Nothing is merged that differs; nothing that is the same is listed twice. This
is not aggregation (no class is combined) and not counting (no chain is weighed); it is identity.
**"Simple"** = no proposition visited twice on a route, so a cycle in a component terminates rather
than multiplying routes, and no claim can repeat on a route.

**The seam was live.** Checked against the build rather than assumed: a correspondence to a
correspondent whose concerns claim has two grounds produced **four chains, two of them the same
ground sequence** — 7C's two paths for that claim (one per ground) each enumerated both grounds.
Exactly Rob's `A → B → C` twice. Fixed in the derivation: `add` keys every chain by its ground
sequence and reports each once; `_correspondence_routes` documents *simple* exactly. Fixture
**Ms27**: the two-ground terminal → exactly two chains (both undetermined, through correspondence;
their terminal links knowing and not_knowing); a second correspondence claim between the same pair
→ four chains, all distinct sequences; a cycle `J ~ Jb ~ Jc ~ J` → three routes to Jb, no
proposition twice, six distinct chains; and a corpus-wide check that no report lists one ground
sequence twice — enforced, not assumed. Smoke s111 gains a thirteenth mutation (*the same chain
enumerated twice through equivalent traversals*), caught. One expectation in the new check was
written wrong on the first run (chains through correspondence are undetermined by design, not
knowing / not_knowing — the terminal link's classes are what differ); the check was corrected, the
derivation was not.

**What 7F inherits, stated:** a chain's identity is its ground sequence; a route is simple; equal
sequences are one chain; distinct claims are distinct chains. 7F traverses ruled relations, not
about-ness chains, and may not reinterpret this seam. Gate 1206 → 1208, 0 failed.
