# Groundwork - what it is

Groundwork is a personal-finance program for a household's own computer (Windows, macOS, Linux). It keeps the
household's record of money - accounts, transactions, budget, debts, savings goals, investments, medical bills - and
turns that record into explanations in the household's own numbers.

## Who it is for

Households who want to understand where their money goes and what their choices change - including people under
financial strain, couples who share money, and people who distrust finance apps that want their bank login or sell
their data.

## What it promises

- **Local and private.** The household's financial data is stored only on their computer; there is no Groundwork
  account and no cloud copy, and the app sends no telemetry, analytics or usage tracking. A few features ask the
  outside world for a public fact, and none of those requests carries balances, transactions, amounts, names or
  account details: the update check, investment prices (the ticker symbols held), utility and fuel prices (the
  state), trip distances (the places typed), and public fare and per-diem tables.
- **Getting the data in.** Transactions come from files the person downloads from their bank (CSV, OFX, QFX, QIF and
  others), from the bank's own website opened in a window inside Groundwork, on the person's click, or by hand.
- **Free, with nothing to sell.** No subscription, ads or upsell, so nothing pushes the person toward a product.
- **Optional local AI.** A small AI model can run on the computer itself to phrase explanations. It is the person's
  choice to turn on, and every feature works with it off. It only words figures Groundwork has already worked out;
  it is not allowed to invent numbers, and a check blocks sentences that do.
- **Every figure from the record.** A number on screen comes from the person's own records, and pages say where it
  comes from ("From your ledger this month..."). Where the record can't support a figure, Groundwork says so rather
  than guessing.
- **Shows, doesn't scold.** Pages state what happened and what would change it. They don't moralise, block purchases,
  or say what the person "should" do.
- **Honest options only.** When something doesn't fit, the options it offers work within the money the household
  already has - moving money between budget lines, timing, payoff order, trimming named costs, saving first. It never
  suggests "earn more", a new loan, a balance transfer or buy-now-pay-later. When nothing fits, it says so plainly and
  shows the nearest real path.
- **The person's word stands.** Groundwork can draw a few conclusions from what the person did to their own budget
  (for example, a budget cut that held). The person can answer any of them, and what they say always outranks what
  Groundwork read. It never draws conclusions from how often the app is opened or which suggestions were dismissed.
- **Research, with its limits.** Some pages show a published research finding next to the household's own figures,
  each with what the study does not show and its source.

## The main pages

- **Dashboard** - this month so far, what needs attention, what changed since last month.
- **Ledger** and **Budget** - the transactions, and the plan for each category.
- **Working picture** - what Groundwork has concluded from the household's own budget edits, what they have told it,
  questions where the two disagree, and research beside their figures.
- **Debt Payoff**, **Investments**, **Net Worth** - each debt's path to zero, holdings, and the balance sheet over time.
- **Before You Buy** and **Trip Planner** - what a purchase or a trip does to the plan before it happens.
- **Health** - medical bills as the provider sent them (owed only once the person says so), the plan year, a reserve.
- **Reports**, **Trophy Room**, **Assistant**, **Settings**.
