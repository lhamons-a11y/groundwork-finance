# Prompt: simulate user groups reviewing Groundwork

Paste this into a chat together with `BRIEF.md` and the 15 screenshots in this folder.

---

You are running a structured usability and fit study of **Groundwork**, a free, local-only personal-finance app. Read
`BRIEF.md` first. It explains what the app is, the rules it holds itself to, the made-up sample household (the
Morgans) and what each screenshot shows. Then study the screenshots.

## The user groups

Simulate each group below as **three distinct people**. Give each a name, an age, an income, their debts, their
comfort with technology, and one money worry in their own words. Keep them realistic and varied. No one is a caricature.

1. **New graduate with student loans.** First real paycheck, a credit card balance creeping up.
2. **Single parent living paycheck to paycheck.** Irregular overtime, a car loan, little slack in any month.
3. **Couple in active debt payoff,** like the Morgans: two incomes, three debts, arguing a little about restaurants.
4. **Gig or freelance worker with irregular income.** Several platforms, taxes not withheld, months that swing.
5. **Someone in financial crisis.** Medical bills, one account in collections, stressed and ashamed to look.
6. **Retiree on a fixed income.** Social Security and a small pension, careful, wary of software, low vision.
7. **Steady earner starting to invest.** No high-interest debt, a 401(k), and a brokerage account opened this year.
8. **Privacy-first user.** Has refused Mint, YNAB and bank aggregators because of data sharing.
9. **Person with ADHD or easily overwhelmed.** Has tried budgeting apps and quit each within two weeks.

## For each person

Walk through the screens as that person would on their first week with their own data, using the Morgans as a
stand-in for what their screens would look like. Say:

1. **First impression** (Dashboard): what they understand in 10 seconds, and what confuses them.
2. **The three pages they would use most,** and why.
3. **What helps:** a specific screen and element, and the reason it helps *this* person.
4. **What doesn't work:** a specific screen and element, what goes wrong for this person (confusion, overwhelm,
   distrust, missing feature, wrong assumption), and how bad it is: blocker, major or minor.
5. **Tone:** quote any wording that would feel judgmental, preachy, cold or falsely cheerful to them, and how they
   would take it.
6. **Trust:** do they believe the numbers? Do they believe the privacy claim? What would raise or lower their trust?
7. **The AI parts** (the Projection, Explain, the Ask bar, report reads): useful, ignored, or harmful for them? Check
   every AI sentence against the numbers on the same screen and flag anything made up.
8. **Would they still be using it after 30 days?** Yes or no, and the one thing that decides it.

## Then, across all the groups

- **Top 10 things that work,** ranked by how many groups they help, with the screens.
- **Top 10 problems,** ranked by severity times how many groups they hit, each with:
  - the screen and the exact element or wording;
  - which groups it hits;
  - a concrete change. Say what should change, not "improve the UX".
- **Rule check:** every place on any screen that breaks one of the rules in `BRIEF.md` (math-true or silent, no new
  debt, no "earn more", show-don't-scold, proof not persuasion, rarely speaks first, asks rather than assumes, the AI
  only phrases). Quote it.
- **Missing for each group:** what one feature would most change their outcome, if it fits the rules.
- **Accessibility:** contrast, text size, density, jargon and reading level, keyboard use, colour-only signals.
- **Confirm or dispute** each item in the brief's "Known issues", and add any the brief missed.

## Ground rules for the study

- Stay inside what the screenshots and the brief show. When something can't be judged from them, say *can't tell
  from these screens* rather than guessing how it works.
- No product pitch, and no flattery. Disagreement and problems are the useful part.
- Write plainly, in short sentences, with specific screen names and quoted wording.
